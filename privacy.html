TaskSnap privacy policy

Last updated: October 23, 2026

TaskSnap helps you turn photos of things you'd like to change into small, doable tasks. We built it so your tasks and photos stay yours. This policy explains, in plain English, what the app stores, where it goes and what choices you have.

The short version

  • Your photos and tasks are stored on your device.
  • If you turn on iCloud sync, they are also stored in your own iCloud account. We can't see them there.
  • We don't run analytics, we don't show ads and we don't track you across apps or websites.
  • The "small steps from your photo" feature runs entirely on your device.
  • A few optional features (household sharing and focus rooms) share limited information with other people. They're explained below.

Who we are

TaskSnap is made by Warner Gears ("we", "us"). If you have a question about this policy or your data, email us at samvaiotmp@icloud.com.

What TaskSnap stores, and where

Photos and tasks: on your device

The photos you take in TaskSnap (before and after photos) and the tasks you create (titles, notes, steps, categories, due dates, tags, templates, streaks and achievements) are saved on your iPhone or iPad. They don't leave your device unless you turn on one of the features below.

TaskSnap uses the camera only when you choose to take a photo. It does not scan the rest of your photo library. Any image you choose to share or save is handled through the controls you use.

iCloud sync (optional)

If you turn on iCloud sync, TaskSnap uses Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) to keep your tasks and photos up to date on your devices. This data is stored in the private part of your iCloud account, under Apple's privacy policy. We have no access to it. You can turn sync off at any time in Settings. Your data then stays on each device.

Backups (optional)

If you make a backup in Settings, TaskSnap saves a backup file to your iCloud Drive when it's available, or to the app's storage on your device when it isn't. You can make, restore or delete backups in Settings. If you enable automatic backups, the app also creates backups according to that setting. Backup files can include your task photos; protect any copies you export.

Exports (optional)

If you export your data (for example as CSV or JSON), TaskSnap creates the file on your device and hands it to the iOS share sheet. You decide where it goes.

Small steps from your photo (on-device AI)

TaskSnap can suggest a few small steps for a task. This happens on your device:

  • Apple's Vision framework looks at the photo on your device to describe what's in it (for example "desk" or "laundry").
  • On devices with Apple Intelligence, Apple's on-device Foundation Models framework writes the steps from those descriptions and your task title. The photo itself is not given to the model.
  • On other devices, TaskSnap picks steps using built-in rules.

Either way, nothing is sent to us or to any server for this feature. Category suggestions work the same way, on your device.

Notifications

Reminders (for example for a due task or your streak) are scheduled by TaskSnap on your device using local notifications. You can turn them off in Settings or in iOS Settings. When iCloud sync or household sharing is on, iCloud may send your device silent background updates so shared changes arrive. These contain no message from us.

Subscriptions (TaskSnap Pro)

Purchases are handled by Apple through the App Store. We never see your name, Apple ID or payment details. The app checks with Apple's StoreKit on your device whether you have an active subscription. Apple may share limited, non-identifying sales reports with us, as it does for every developer. You can manage or cancel your subscription in your Apple account settings.

Features that share information with other people

These features are optional. They only share anything when you use them.

Household sharing

If you create or join a household, the tasks and photos in that household are shared through iCloud with the people you invite. They can see and change those tasks. TaskSnap shows who claimed or finished a task using the name iCloud provides for each person. Your other tasks stay private. You can leave a household or stop sharing at any time. Shared data is stored in iCloud under Apple's privacy policy, and we can't see it.

Focus rooms (body doubling)

If you join a focus room, TaskSnap puts a small presence record in a room that other TaskSnap users can see. It's stored in Apple's CloudKit public database for the app. The record includes:

  • a display name (a random name like "User 1234" unless you set your own)
  • a symbol for your avatar
  • the name of the task you're focusing on, if you chose one
  • your session goal, if you add one
  • your session start and end times, activity status and last update time
  • your reactions
  • a random identifier that iCloud gives the app (not your Apple ID or email)

Session records are marked inactive when you leave. Presence records stop appearing in the active room after their heartbeat becomes stale. Leaving a room does not automatically delete the stored session or presence record; there is no automatic retention deadline in the current version. Avoid entering sensitive information in a shared room. If you'd rather not share a task name, start a session without picking a task.

What we don't do

  • No analytics or tracking SDKs, no advertising and no data brokers.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites (as defined by Apple's App Tracking Transparency).
  • We don't sell or rent your information.
  • TaskSnap has an optional on-device usage summary in Settings > Diagnostics. It's off unless you turn it on, it never leaves your device, and you can reset it at any time.

Support, website hosting and TestFlight

If you email support, we receive your email address and whatever you include. We use it to answer your request and troubleshoot the app. Do not send passwords, payment information or private task photos unless you intend to share them. You can ask us to delete support correspondence, subject to any records we need to retain for legal obligations.

These support pages are hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub may process technical request information, such as your IP address, to deliver and secure the site, under its privacy statement. These pages do not use analytics, advertising, cookies or contact forms of our own.

When you use a TestFlight build, Apple processes beta testing data and can provide the developer with crash reports, testing information and feedback you choose to send, including screenshots and contact details. This is separate from the app's on-device diagnostics setting. See Apple's TestFlight privacy information. We use beta reports to fix problems and improve the app.

Children

TaskSnap isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

Keeping or deleting your data

  • To delete a task or photo, delete it in the app. This does not delete copies in existing backups, exports or another person’s files; remove those separately where you control them.
  • To delete everything on a device, delete the app. If iCloud sync is on, you can also remove TaskSnap's data from your iCloud account in iOS Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Manage Account Storage.
  • To remove focus room records or ask us anything about your data, email samvaiotmp@icloud.com.

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR or CCPA), you may have rights to access, correct or delete your personal information. Because almost everything stays on your device or in your own iCloud account, you can usually do this yourself in the app. For anything else, email us and we'll help.

Changes to this policy

If we change how TaskSnap handles your information, we'll update this page and the date at the top. For a meaningful change, we'll also tell you in the app.
